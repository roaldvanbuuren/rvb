The AI revolution in games has scared many, but gaming might actually be one of the industries best prepared to adopt and implement it at scale, because it's not the first time tech was commoditized and story, taste, and IP became a central USP.

That is because of the game engines. Back in 90s and early 00s, games would build their own infrastructure and technical capabilities were the main bottleneck and decider if a game was successful or not. But in the late 00s and 10s, game engines took over. This meant that development became a commodity, and the story your game told, world building, art style, characters, and creative direction became what determined success.

AI takes that a step further. It is easier than ever to create a game, and what will set it apart is what you build with it. How culturally relevant is it, the creative direction, deciding what actually stays in the game, what gets cut, and keeping a clear vision when generating options costs nearly nothing.

Right now games is also in a transitional period. Many games are unfortunately being copied, game studios, especially indies, are still scared to implement it because of community backlash, but also have to because every other studio is doing it. These are issues that will be smoothened out, partly through regulation (Anthropic themselves are cracking down already on it), partly because players will get used to it over time.

Lastly, here in Europe the discussion around owning a frontier model continues, and remains important, especially for enterprise, infrastructure and sovereignty, yet the games industries show that you don't have to own the underlying infra in order to succeed, and that for consumer businesses, owning and understanding the consumer and cultural layer is more important.

While the most successful engines are US-owned (Unity, Unreal), many of the most successful games are European, such as GTA, Red Dead Redemption, The Witcher, Cyberpunk 2077, Kingdom Come Deliverance, Baldur's Gate, Arc Raiders, and plenty others.

So in short; AI makes technology more accessible than ever, so if you understand what drives consumer culture, and ensure creative approaches, you can still win without owning the underlying technology stack.
