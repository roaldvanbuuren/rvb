In gaming, we love to talk about community, and how important it is. But recently, I have been speaking with quite a few stakeholders in sports, where it has also become a buzzy word. I see one big difference. In gaming we speak a lot about it, but thats often where it stops. It is usually something that is formed by the players themselves, rather than crafted by the publishers. It is not actioned until the last moment before launch of a title, seen as something that occurs on Discord, and/or passed off to a junior.

In sports, community is a boardroom conversation, because there is an understanding that new audiences are limited, so you need to make the most of what you have.

To an extent, it is logical this is the case in modern gaming; the focus is always on the next release, rather than the current game, so there is more a sense of again reaching new audiences.

Funnily enough though, those that do take community seriously, reap the benefits of having games that last for decades. Counter-Strike (25+ years), Roblox (20), League of Legends (17).

So maybe there is a lesson in sport; stop always thinking about your next audience. Treat the players you already have well - bring them in early, actually listen to them, and make them feel like its partly their game too - and you’ll reap the benefit. LTV will go up and they’ll help with UA, both for your current title, as well as your future releases.
