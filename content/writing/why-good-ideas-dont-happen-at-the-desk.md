With the whole grindslop discussion firing up because of the Harry Stebbing interview with the Corgi founder, it shows that science, psychology and human biology often get thrown out the window (hi 996). The reality for most is that good ideas rarely happen behind a desk. They tend to happen during gaming sessions, going for a walk, or chores that you do on auto (hi shower thought).

And there’s good reason (and real science) behind this.

A friend recently explained me about two distinct mental states:

- The Info-Gathering Mind: When you’re at your desk, in meetings, networking, etc. You are consuming data, but you aren’t actually using it yet. Most founders, such as the Corgi founder, are stuck in this state.

- Then there’s the Processing Mind: When you’re doing chores, playing games or on autopilot. This is when your brain connects the dots and puts that data into perspective. This is where creative breakthroughs happen that unlock the other mode to be used with more purpose and efficiency.

As a personal anecdote, end of last year I took a Thursday. Slack off, no emails, no calls - only games, reading and thinking. That one day triggered ELO’s (my business) biggest pivot, moving away from being service oriented to building products for community building and engagement. This would have never happened had I not given my brain the space to connect the dots I had been gathering the months prior to that.

My advice here is, don’t fall for the 996 pressure. As a founder, managing your team, processes, meetings, networking, fundraising, etc are all important, but they are so much less impactful if you don’t give your brain a moment to process all the data.
