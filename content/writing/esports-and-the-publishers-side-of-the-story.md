Pointing to publishers as the issue against sustainable growth in esports has been the go-to excuse for a decade+. “Publishers should do more to grow esports”. I’ve said it myself too often.

But I want to propose another side, the publisher’s side, which raises the question of “why would I?”.

If an esports org or tournament would disappear tomorrow, would it make a dent in the publisher’s revenue? In most cases, the answer will be no. The game will keep selling skins, the org or TO gets replaced by another, or esports gets replaced by a whole other way of driving engagement. #Esports holds no real leverage against the publisher to ensure there is alignment that benefits the esports ecosystems to the publisher’s.

Yet we keep making demands from a position of dependancy, but that leads us nowhere sustainable.

The better framing might just be “what can we offer that the publisher can’t get anywhere else?”: Reach into communities they can’t access, retention they can’t build, cultural alignment with players they can’t buy. Until we can answer that question convincingly, “publishers should do more” is just a complaint, not a strategy.

This starts with teams, players and TOs aligning and working together to create paths to leverage, rather than all work against another.
