Yesterday night there was a nation wide celebration happening in Serbia. After years of protests, the sitting government was out after democratic elections. The catalyst of the protests was an incident where a train station canopy collapsed and 16 people died. Students amassed and protests for 2 years using the incident as a consequence of years of state corruption.

This news will most likely bolster many of the protestors (and unfortunately, rioters) to continue their efforts in other European cities.

But while there are plenty of valid reasons to protest against European leaders (decades of underhand dealings, bureaucracy, misspending, etc etc etc), the more western uprisings seem to lack one thing: A shared identity and goal. It all kicked off with French protests around lack of education funding, which spiralled into calls about corruption, fascism, police states, cost of living and rising oil prices, with sprinkles of Antifa and Palestinian protests on top. On their own, each of these may be a valid reason to protest. All together, they present a mix of everything and nothingness, inner-protest confusion, and.. riots. There is a clear lack of cohesion in the message, which shows a lack of real leadership in this movement to drive on message throughout cities and nations.

It’ll be an interesting time to see how things begin to unfold, and if the Serbian protestors’ success is the fall of the first domino, or a fluke. After all, it wouldn’t be the first time a balkan state triggered unthinkable ripples throughout Europe and the world.
