In most of Europe, the narrative is that Brexit was a catastrophic failure, which was always going to end up with the UK begging to come back, or endless suffering. Yesterday I was having a discussion with a Dutch friend around exactly that.

Having lived through the Brexit-vote and period predating and following it, now being in Italy, and originally Dutch and pro-EU, my view is a bit more nuanced than most other European mainlanders. At the time of Brexit, I might have been one of the few of those in London who could understand “the other side”. The EU adds a lot of difficulty, and the unity is not always best for the individual nation. For a nation as proud and heritage rich as Britain, many things were always going to be tough to swallow. Especially with the narrative that was created by Bojo and his bunch. So while at the time I may not have agreed, I did understand.

And once it was done, my belief was that it could be successful, if Britain went all-in. Now, as expected, there was endless back and forths, a merry-go-round of PMs, and decisions pulling the nation many different ways. Add a sprinkle of COVID on top of that and the war in Ukraine, and you have the recipe for disaster, which ensued as expected.

The promised Singapore-on-Thames never happened, because there was never a real plan for it. But that opportunity for a Singapore-like position, between EU / Canada, and the US, still exists. Perhaps stronger than ever, due to the continued tensions inside the West and NATO, and the heralding age of AI.

I read an interesting piece by @discoplomacy the other day (link in added comment), that spoke about the opportunity for Britain for AI. The gist: Britain can’t outspend Silicon Valley or outbuild China, so it’s building institutions around the frontier. First the AI Security Institute (since copied by a bunch of other countries), and now the AI Biosecurity Lab, with £100m from government, plus up to £100m each from OpenAI and Anthropic, with Google DeepMind on board too. The UK brings the science, the talent and the trust. The US labs bring the models.

I think this is what a Singapore-like position actually looks like in practice. Close enough to the US to get access to the best models, close enough to Europe to work with what we have here (ASML being the obvious one, but also imec’s chip research, the exascale supercomputer in Germany, and universities and talent), and trusted enough that both sides want to work with you.

It probably won’t make headlines the way the “Brexit dividend” did, and tbh it’s not the Brexit that was sold. But it’s the first time in a while I’ve seen the UK pick a lane and go all-in on it.

Curious if others see it the same way, especially my European friends.
