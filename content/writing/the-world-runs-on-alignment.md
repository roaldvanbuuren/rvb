Most people don't realize the world runs on one thing: alignment.

Business, government, geopolitics, even your own relationship. The most successful ones have one thing in common: alignment.

Alignment does not mean agreeing how it should be done A-Z, but there needs to be a shared, common goal. Differences on the way there can then be overcome, and middlegrounds can be found much easier.

We come off the back of one of the greatest eras of alignment, the one of the West. Where post-WW2, there were certain agreements and goals to ensure the same would never happen again. Were the means always agreed upon? Most definitely not. But the ends were always aligned.

So why does the world seem so chaotic now then? Because the alignment has been interrupted. Since the dawn of social media, the means have begun to outshine the end. How things were done, which was not always cleanly, were brought to light, and exploited by oppositions. The mainstream media, in a drive for survival, followed this example.

Our democratic system did not help with this. Four- or five-year cycles are not enough to reach the ends. Those tend to take decades to establish. So the means have become important, because those can show short-term impact, even though they may be detrimental in the long term.

Why was Brexit successful while Remain floundered? There was a clear message for Brexit, one that people rallied behind, both politically and individually. Remain lacked that.

As a European, I was hopeful that at least one good thing could come out of the Ukraine-Russia War; European alignment. Add on top an adversary from the other side of the ocean (Trump), and you'd think you'd have a perfect mix for alignment.

Unfortunately, personal and national interests, bureaucracy, weak leadership and messaging, all seem too strong to use this tragic but unique opportunity to our benefit.

Even in the riots that took hold in European cities over the last weeks, they seem not to have taken hold. What was missing? Alignment. The anger was shared, but it never became a shared goal. One city's uprising was against fascism, the next against tuition fees, the other against education budgets, and another against cost of petrol. There was no aligned goal that all could rally behind.

So if there is something where you see a shift or a change in posture, by businesses, institutions, or governments, look at alignment; whether that has been lost or gained. The entire NVIDIA/Jensen Huang saga is an interesting one to follow on this topic right now. Who did he never align with, and who is lining up now?
