One of Europe’s big issues is that wealth sits either still on bank accounts or in outdated vehicles. Yet at the same time we keep complaining that our best founders have to go to the US to raise.

While Draghi’s report says the same thing, his fix is mostly plumbing around capital markets union and pension funds. I think we could be a bit more direct. Put a small negative rate on large personal cash balances, say above €250k, and at the same time give proper tax relief to anyone who backs European startups as an angel or an LP, like the UK does with SEIS (up to 50% of the investment back as income tax relief).

Might sound crazy but the ECB ran negative rates from 2014 to 2022 and plenty of banks passed them on to big savers, and the banking system didn’t collapse (the money mostly went into property and stocks instead, which is kind of the point, you’d want it going somewhere more useful).

I’m not talking about someone’s pension or their house deposit, just the big piles doing nothing.

Curious if anyone with a policy background thinks this is naive or actually doable.
